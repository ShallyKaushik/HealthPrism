HEART HEALTHY DIET
To manage high cardiovascular risk, patients should limit sodium intake to under 1,500 mg per day. Saturated fats should be restricted to less than 6% of total daily calories. 
Recommended foods for heart health include oats, beans, fatty fish (like salmon and mackerel due to omega-3s), nuts, and leafy green vegetables.
Avoid processed meats, excessive alcohol, and refined carbohydrates. Opt for whole grains over white flour.

WEIGHT LOSS GUIDELINES
A healthy rate of weight loss is 1 to 2 pounds per week. This generally requires a daily caloric deficit of 500 to 1,000 calories.
High-protein diets can help preserve lean muscle mass during weight loss and increase satiety. Excellent sources of lean protein include chicken breast, turkey, tofu, lentils, Greek yogurt, and eggs.
Increase dietary fiber through fruits, vegetables, and legumes to feel fuller for longer. Maintain strong hydration by drinking at least 8 glasses of water daily.

MACRONUTRIENT RANGES
A balanced diet typically consists of 45-65% carbohydrates, 10-35% protein, and 20-35% fat. 
Active individuals or those trying to lose weight may benefit from shifting to the higher end of the protein range (up to 30-35%) while lowering carbohydrate intake.
Individuals with high cholesterol should ensure their fat intake comes primarily from monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fats (avocados, olive oil, almonds) rather than saturated fats (butter, red meat).

LOW SODIUM LIFESTYLE
For individuals suffering from high resting blood pressure (hypertension), sodium is the primary dietary antagonist. 
Flavor foods with herbs, spices, lemon, lime, or vinegar instead of salt. 
Beware of "hidden" sodium in canned soups, frozen meals, and condiments like soy sauce or ketchup. Always choose "low sodium" or "no salt added" variants when buying canned goods.

DIABETES AND BLOOD SUGAR MANAGEMENT
Patients with diabetes or pre-diabetes should prioritize foods with a low glycemic index (GI).
Pair carbohydrates with a fat or protein source to slow digestion and prevent rapid blood sugar spikes.
Avoid sugary beverages entirely; replace soda and juices with water, unsweetened tea, or black coffee.

GENERAL WELLNESS AND STRESS REDUCTION
Diet plays a major role in chronic stress and fatigue. Complex carbohydrates (like oatmeal) can help the brain make more serotonin. 
Vitamin C helps lower cortisol levels. Foods rich in Vitamin C include citrus fruits, strawberries, and bell peppers.
Magnesium acts as a natural muscle relaxant. Increase magnesium intake through spinach, pumpkin seeds, and black beans.
